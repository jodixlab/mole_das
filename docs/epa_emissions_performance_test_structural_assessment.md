# EPA Emissions Performance Test Structural Assessment

## Purpose

This document consolidates the EPA guidance and regulatory sources most useful for a **structural assessment** of an emissions performance test package. It is organized to answer a practical review question:

> **What elements must appear in an EPA-style emissions performance test plan and final report, and which of those elements are guidance-based versus rule-based?**

This document treats the following as the core authorities:

1. **EPA EMC GD-042, _Preparation and Review of Site-Specific Emission Test Plans_** — primary EPA format guide for the pretest package.[^gd42-preface]
2. **EPA EMC GD-043, _Preparation and Review of Emission Test Reports_** — primary EPA format guide for the final test report.[^gd43-purpose]
3. **40 CFR 60.8, 61.13, and 63.7** — binding regulatory provisions for NSPS, Part 61 NESHAP, and Part 63 MACT/NESHAP performance testing.[^cfr60-report][^cfr61-notice][^cfr63-plan]
4. **EPA Clean Air Act National Stack Testing Guidance** — policy guidance on consistency, oversight, and stack-test conduct.[^stack-guidance]
5. **EPA electronic reporting pages for ERT/CEDRI** — modern reporting context for stationary-source test plans and reports.[^epa-electronic-reporting][^epa-ert]

---

## Executive conclusion

For a structural review, the cleanest EPA framework is:

- Use **GD-042** to assess whether the **site-specific test plan** is complete and reviewable.[^gd42-preface]
- Use **GD-043** to assess whether the **final emissions test report** is complete and reviewable.[^gd43-purpose]
- Use **40 CFR 60.8 / 61.13 / 63.7** to determine which items are not merely good practice but **legally required** for the applicable program.[^cfr60-report][^cfr61-notice][^cfr63-plan]
- Then overlay the **subpart-specific rule** and the **permit language**, because many source categories modify defaults such as number of runs, representative load, notifications, or electronic submittal method.[^cfr60-three-runs][^epa-ert]

## 1. Core EPA structural sources

### 1.1 GD-042 — site-specific emission test plans

EPA states that GD-042 presents a **standard format** for preparing a test plan, and that the standard test plan contains a **table of contents, nine sections, and appendices if needed**.[^gd42-preface]

The document table of contents identifies the nine sections as follows:[^gd42-toc]

1. Introduction
2. Source Description
3. Test Program
4. Sampling Locations
5. Sampling and Analytical Procedures
6. Internal QA/QC Activities
7. Reporting and Data Reduction Requirements
8. Plant Entry and Safety
9. Personnel Responsibilities and Test Schedule

### 1.2 GD-043 — emission test reports

EPA states that GD-043 presents a **standard format** for preparing the emissions test report, and that the standard report contains a **table of contents, six sections, and appendices**.[^gd43-purpose]

The GD-043 table of contents identifies the principal sections as follows:[^gd43-toc]

1. Introduction
2. Plant and Sampling Location Description
3. Summary and Discussion of Results
4. Sampling and Analytical Procedures
5. QA/QC Activities
6. Appendices

> Note: GD-043 describes the report structure in five numbered sections plus appendices; EPA’s own text characterizes the overall standard report as containing six sections and appendices.[^gd43-purpose]

### 1.3 National Stack Testing Guidance

EPA’s National Stack Testing Guidance explains that the guidance was developed to improve consistency in how stack tests are conducted for compliance purposes and to address longstanding concerns about inconsistent procedures and inconsistent reporting of results.[^stack-guidance]

### 1.4 Electronic reporting context

EPA’s current electronic reporting page states that the electronic reporting system includes **ERT, CEDRI, and WebFIRE**; that **ERT is used to electronically create stationary source sampling test plans and reports**; and that **CEDRI accepts electronically submitted performance test reports** for applicable Part 60, 62, and 63 programs.[^epa-electronic-reporting]

EPA’s ERT page adds that the ERT format highlights key information required by existing EPA Federal Test Methods, facilitates coordination among the source, tester, and regulator, and standardizes reports.[^epa-ert]

## 2. Structural elements required in a site-specific test plan

## 2.1 High-level structure

A structurally complete EPA-style test plan should contain the nine GD-042 sections listed above, supported by tables, figures, and appendices as needed.[^gd42-preface][^gd42-toc]

## 2.2 Required or expected elements by section

### Section 1 — Introduction

GD-042 identifies the summary of the test program as the opening structural element. In that summary, EPA expects the plan to identify, as applicable, the responsible groups, overall purpose of the test, regulations, industry, plant name and location, processes of interest, control equipment, emission points and sampling locations, pollutants to be measured, and expected test dates.[^gd42-summary]

**Structural takeaway:** the plan should begin with a short administrative and regulatory framing section that answers **who, what, where, why, and when**.

### Section 2 — Source description

GD-042 includes a dedicated source-description section with subsections for the **process description** and **control equipment description**.[^gd42-toc]

**Structural takeaway:** a defensible plan should document:

- process flow and major unit operations,
- the emission unit under test,
- the control device or control train,
- the operating variables that affect emissions,
- and enough source context to interpret representative operating conditions.

### Section 3 — Test program

GD-042 places the **objectives** and **test matrix** in the test-program section.[^gd42-toc]

**Structural takeaway:** the plan should include:

- each pollutant or parameter to be measured,
- each test method,
- each sampling location,
- the number of runs,
- run timing,
- supporting process data to be recorded,
- and the compliance or engineering objective of each test segment.

### Section 4 — Sampling locations

GD-042 devotes a full section to sampling locations and instructs the preparer to provide a schematic of each location, including duct diameter, direction of flow, and distances to upstream and downstream disturbances in duct diameters.[^gd42-sampling-locations]

**Structural takeaway:** the plan should document sampling-location adequacy with enough detail to evaluate method applicability and traverse design.

This dovetails with the CFR facility requirements. Under **40 CFR 60.8(e)**, the owner or operator must provide adequate sampling ports, safe platforms, safe access, utilities, and a stack or duct arrangement that allows flow and emissions to be accurately determined, including a stack or duct free of cyclonic flow when required by the applicable method.[^cfr60-facilities] Under **40 CFR 61.13(d)**, the owner or operator must provide adequate ports, safe platforms, safe access, utilities, and any other facilities necessary for safe and proper testing.[^cfr61-facilities]

### Section 5 — Sampling and analytical procedures

GD-042 lists **test methods** and **process data** under this section.[^gd42-procedures]

**Structural takeaway:** the plan should contain:

- the exact reference methods,
- train configurations and schematics,
- sample recovery approach,
- analytical procedure flow,
- process-data collection procedures,
- method deviations or modifications,
- and any alternative-method approvals if applicable.

This is also reinforced by **40 CFR 60.8(b)**, which requires performance tests and data reduction to be conducted in accordance with the applicable methods and procedures unless the Administrator specifies or approves a modification, equivalent method, alternative method, waiver, or shorter sampling conditions where permitted.[^cfr60-methods]

### Section 6 — Internal QA/QC activities

GD-042 identifies four QA/QC subelements: **QC procedure, QA audits, QA/QC checks for data reduction and validation, and sample identification and custody**.[^gd42-qaqc]

For Part 63 testing, the rule text is explicit: before a required performance test, the owner or operator must develop a site-specific test plan that includes a **test program summary, test schedule, data quality objectives, and both an internal and external QA program**.[^cfr63-plan] Part 63 further explains that the internal QA program must include activities planned by routine operators and analysts to assess test-data precision, and that the performance test must include a performance audit where applicable.[^cfr63-plan][^cfr63-audit]

**Structural takeaway:** QA/QC is not a side note. It is a core structural section.

### Section 7 — Reporting and data reduction requirements

GD-042 includes **report format** and **data reduction and summary** under this section.[^gd42-reporting]

**Structural takeaway:** the test plan should explain in advance:

- how results will be calculated,
- correction bases and units,
- averaging logic,
- compliance comparison basis,
- and the expected structure of summary tables and attachments.

This is especially important because **40 CFR 60.8(f)** establishes a default of **three separate runs** unless an applicable subpart states otherwise.[^cfr60-three-runs]

### Section 8 — Plant entry and safety

GD-042 assigns an entire section to **plant entry and safety**, with subsections for **safety responsibilities, safety program, and safety requirements**.[^gd42-safety]

**Structural takeaway:** the plan should include site-access procedures, safety authority, PPE expectations, restricted areas, permitting requirements, fall protection/access issues, utilities, and emergency communication protocols.

### Section 9 — Personnel responsibilities and test schedule

GD-042 closes with **test site organization** and the overall test schedule.[^gd42-personnel]

**Structural takeaway:** the plan should assign responsibility for field testing, laboratory work, process-data collection, safety, and regulatory coordination, and should identify the execution timeline.

## 3. Structural elements required in the final test report

## 3.1 High-level structure

GD-043 states that the emission test report is the documentation needed to show the objectives of the test and whether proper procedures were used, and that the report format consists of a table of contents, six sections, and appendices.[^gd43-purpose]

## 3.2 Required or expected elements by section

### Section 1 — Introduction

GD-043 begins with a summary of the test program and expects the report to identify responsible groups, overall purpose, regulations, industry, plant name and location, processes of interest, control equipment, emission points and sampling locations, pollutants measured, and dates of testing.[^gd43-summary]

### Section 2 — Plant and sampling location description

GD-043 includes a dedicated plant/sampling-location section and expects the process description to include enough operational detail to understand feed, equipment, schedule, and relevant flow streams.[^gd43-process]

**Structural takeaway:** the report should present the unit, process, controls, and sampling locations in a form that lets a reviewer understand what was actually tested.

### Section 3 — Summary and discussion of results

GD-043 places the **objectives and test matrix** here and expects the report to present the sampling matrix and discuss results in light of the test objectives.[^gd43-objectives]

**Structural takeaway:** the report should include:

- test objective,
- run matrix,
- run dates and times,
- pollutant-by-method mapping,
- observed field changes or anomalies,
- final results and averages,
- and a compliance-oriented narrative discussion.

### Section 4 — Sampling and analytical procedures

GD-043 states that the report should include the procedures actually used, and the example section emphasizes that similar schematics and flow diagrams should be included for each test method.[^gd43-methods]

**Structural takeaway:** the report should document what was actually done, not just what had been planned.

### Section 5 — QA/QC activities

GD-043 includes a full QA/QC section and specifically expects discussion of QA/QC problems encountered.[^gd43-qaqc]

**Structural takeaway:** the report should disclose audit results, recovery/spike issues, sample custody matters, calibration or analytical anomalies, corrective actions, and any impacts on data validity.

### Appendices

A structurally complete report should place the supporting material in appendices, including raw data, calibration records, laboratory data, calculations, chain-of-custody records, field notes, diagrams, and supporting correspondence where relevant.[^gd43-purpose][^cfr60-report]

## 4. Binding CFR elements that should be checked in every review

The EPA guidance documents are the best structural templates, but the CFR establishes the minimum legal baseline.

### 4.1 NSPS — 40 CFR 60.8

For NSPS-style performance tests, a reviewer should verify at least the following:

- **Representative conditions.** Tests must be conducted under representative conditions specified by the Administrator.[^cfr60-representative]
- **Advance notice.** At least **30 days prior notice** must be provided unless another subpart changes the requirement.[^cfr60-notice]
- **Facilities for testing.** Adequate ports, safe platforms, safe access, utilities, and proper flow conditions must be available.[^cfr60-facilities]
- **Three separate runs.** Unless the subpart states otherwise, each performance test consists of **three separate runs**.[^cfr60-three-runs]
- **Report contents.** The report must include the facility identification, purpose of test, description of emission unit, sampling/analysis procedures and modifications, QA procedures/results, process operating conditions, raw support records where required, and identification of the testing company.[^cfr60-report]
- **Performance audit.** The performance test must include a performance audit where applicable.[^cfr60-report]

### 4.2 NESHAP Part 61 — 40 CFR 61.13

For Part 61 testing, a reviewer should verify at least the following:

- **Advance notice.** The owner or operator must notify the Administrator **at least 30 days before** the emission test.[^cfr61-notice]
- **Testing facilities.** Adequate ports, safe platforms, safe access, utilities, and any other facilities needed for safe and proper testing must be provided.[^cfr61-facilities]
- **Test conditions.** Tests must be conducted under conditions specified by the Administrator based on source design and operation.[^cfr61-notice]
- **Performance audit.** The performance testing must include a performance audit during the test where applicable.[^cfr61-notice]

### 4.3 MACT / Part 63 — 40 CFR 63.7

For Part 63 testing, a reviewer should verify at least the following:

- **Notification.** The source must notify the Administrator in writing of the intent to test **at least 60 calendar days** before the performance test is initially scheduled to begin.[^cfr63-notice]
- **Site-specific test plan.** Before testing, the source must develop a site-specific test plan including the **test program summary, test schedule, data quality objectives, and internal and external QA program**.[^cfr63-plan]
- **Performance audit.** The test must include a performance audit during the compliance test where required.[^cfr63-audit]
- **Plan submission timing.** If requested, the source must submit the site-specific plan at least **60 calendar days** before the scheduled test date, simultaneous with the notice of intent or on a mutually agreed date.[^cfr63-submittal]
- **Agency approval cycle.** The Administrator has a regulatory review/disapproval framework for the site-specific test plan.[^cfr63-submittal]

## 6. Practical advice for your review

### If you are reviewing a **test plan**

A plan is structurally weak if it is missing any of the following:

- the regulatory trigger and objective,
- a clear test matrix,
- sampling-location documentation,
- method-level procedural detail,
- QA/QC design,
- data-reduction framework,
- safety/access provisions,
- or assignment of roles and schedule.

### If you are reviewing a **final report**

A report is structurally weak if it does not let you answer:

- what unit was tested,
- why it was tested,
- under what conditions it was tested,
- what exact methods were used,
- whether QA/QC was acceptable,
- what the raw support record consists of,
- and how the compliance result was calculated.

### Most important legal caution

GD-042 and GD-043 are excellent EPA **format and completeness guides**, but they are not substitutes for the **applicable subpart** and the **permit**. A structurally perfect report can still be noncompliant if the subpart requires different run counts, load conditions, notification timing, or electronic submission procedures.[^cfr60-three-runs][^cfr63-notice][^epa-ert]

- EPA states the site-specific test plan is the foundation for the test report and that the standard test plan contains a table of contents, nine sections, and appendices if needed.[^gd42-preface]
